## Outcome

Transactional mail sent through the production sender at `useqori.com` reaches
a real mailbox. Until today the plan's standing headline was that mail sends
and **none is confirmed arriving** — configuration was known good and delivery
was entirely unproven, which meant every access grant, magic link and
verification email in the product rested on an assumption. That assumption is
now tested at least once, end to end, outside the local inbox.

This unblocks `T-043` (inviting people to a Series), which was waiting on mail
that works.

**Could not verify:** everything the scope asked for beyond arrival itself.
Specifically, and each of these is genuinely open rather than assumed:

- **Which receiving providers were used.** The scope named at least two, and
  called out Gmail and Outlook as the two that matter commercially and behave
  differently. If only one was tested, arrival is proven for one receiving
  stack.
- **Whether SPF, DKIM and DMARC passed on what arrived.** This is the check
  that distinguishes "it arrived" from "it arrived and will keep arriving".
  Mail can land in an inbox today on sender reputation alone and start landing
  in spam once volume rises.
- **Whether all six transactional messages were sent, or a sample.**
- **Inbox versus spam placement.** Arrival in a spam folder is arrival.

None of this makes the outcome wrong. It bounds it: **delivery is proven,
deliverability is not.**
