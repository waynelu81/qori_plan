# T-172 — report

## Outcome

Qori now creates its own plan prices in Stripe. Saving a price in the console
publishes it before the row is saved:
- the price is in AUD, with USD and the other fixed currencies as options;
- it sits on a product per plan with a fixed id, `qori_plan_start`, and the
  lookup key moves onto it;
- the price it replaces is archived, and the new id is stored.

A refusal saves nothing, and staff no longer type a `price_…` id. Stripe lets
no amount be edited, so every change is a new price, and existing subscribers
stay on theirs. The hybrid works end to end in the sandbox: a US buyer sees
US$39.00, and a Japanese buyer sees yen converted by Adaptive Pricing.

## Departures

- The product carries `metadata[qori_plan]`.
- The Stripe tests use `Sleep::fake()`, because a faked 404 or 500 is retried.
- The spike left real test-mode prices in the owner's sandbox (above). They
  are what Qori's own console would have published.

## Found, not fixed

- An Adaptive-priced renewal can charge a different yen amount. Stripe's terms
  on that page say "the exchange rate at the time of billing", and the rate
  carried Stripe's fee: 116.40 against the ECB's 112.25 that day → T-170
- Local development's database needs `php artisan migrate` for `T-169`'s
  columns, and its `subscription_prices` rows still point at the old
  hand-made sandbox prices (US$44 and US$99). Re-saving each plan in the local
  console publishes it → decided: a one-time step after pulling, told to the owner
- `D-048`'s 24-hour idempotency window is Stripe's documented behaviour, not
  observed → decided: the key is unique per publish, so the window cannot bite
