# Review brief — the storage stream, 20 September 2026

**For a developer who did not write it.** Requested by the owner on
20 September 2026, the day `D-036` reversed the Google Drive design. Point a
reviewer at this file and it is the whole instruction: read it, do what it
says, and write the findings into a sibling file (see
[`README.md`](README.md)).

## What you are reviewing

Twenty planning documents specifying how a creator's own files and live
sessions reach the Peers who bought access — Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive,
Vimeo and YouTube, Zoom and Teams. Almost none of it is built. **This is a
review of specifications, not of code**, and the cost of a mistake here is a
month of somebody building the wrong thing.

Read in this order:

1. [`CLAUDE.md`](../../../CLAUDE.md) and [`PROCESS.md`](../PROCESS.md) — the
   conventions, and how a task is claimed, frozen and finished. Read these
   first or nothing else parses.
2. [`PLAN.md`](../../../PLAN.md) — what blocks beta.
3. [`streams/storage.md`](../streams/storage.md) — the stream, its order and
   its reasons.
4. [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md), `D-016` to `D-038` — the settled
   rationale. `D-036`, 20 September 2026, reverses `D-016` for Google Drive.
5. [`reports/T-093-2026-09-20-wayne.md`](../tasks/reports/T-093-2026-09-20-wayne.md)
   and [`tests/Fixtures/google/README.md`](../../../tests/Fixtures/google/README.md)
   — the one place in this stream where a vendor's real behaviour has been
   observed, and the 66 responses it captured.
6. The tasks, `T-091`, `T-094`, `T-092`, `T-044` and `T-089` first.
   `grep -l "^stream: storage" docs/planning/tasks/T-*.md` lists all twenty.

`php artisan qori:tasks` renders the board locally; `--check` applies the

## Aim at these first

They are where the design is load-bearing and least examined. Most of it was
written by one person with an assistant, so an outside reading is the only
thing that will find these.

1. **The fan-out.** A grant is now one vendor call per Peer per Episode file.
   Twenty Episodes and three hundred Peers is six thousand calls at roughly
   0.8 seconds each, measured in `T-093`. `T-091` bounds the inline work at
   `INLINE_GRANT_LIMIT = 5` and leaves the rest to the
   `qori:access:reconcile` sweep. Note that the queue driver is `deferred`
   and nothing is `ShouldQueue`, so the sweep is a scheduled command with no
   retry. Does a Peer who buys on Monday open Episode 14 on Monday? Check the
   arithmetic, and check the vendors' own rate limits, which nobody has
   checked at all.
2. **Every path that ends access.** Refund (`T-103`), Peer removed, Episode
   deleted, Series deleted, Group deleted, provider disconnected, plan
   downgraded. Trace each and say whether it reaches `revokeItem()`. A miss
   leaves a stranger with permanent access to a creator's private file, which
   is the worst thing this stream can do.
3. **Payment taken, grant impossible.** `T-093` saw a creator's token die and
   return `invalid_grant`. A buyer pays at 2am, the grant fails, the state is
   `needs_creator`, the creator is asleep. What does the buyer see, and is
   there a way out? `PLAN.md`'s second execution rule says fulfilment cannot
   be refused after payment.
4. **The 600-Peer cap on one Drive file.** The copy tells a creator that a
   bigger Series "needs a second copy of the file", and the buyer at 601 is
   told to ask the creator to make room. Is that an answer, or a dead end in
   a sentence? Nothing copies a file today, and `D-038` forbids Qori deleting
   one.
5. **Identity.** `T-092` has a Peer confirm the Google account they will open
   with, which may differ from the address they signed up with. Find any path
   where a grant could be made to the wrong address, and say what happens
   when a Peer changes accounts after buying.
6. **Two grant models.** `T-091` now carries grants on a container _and_ on
   an item, because Drive moved to per-file while Dropbox and OneDrive are
   still specified on folders — and their spikes, `T-095` and `T-097`, have
   not run. Is that correct hedging, or premature generality that should
   collapse to one model?
7. **Timeouts.** `D-034` sets the budgets and `T-093` measured real seconds
   per call. Check one against the other, including the slow tail, and
   especially anywhere a vendor call sits inside a person's request.
8. **Fixture fidelity.** The rule here is that no spec may name a vendor
   field that a committed fixture does not show. Check `T-094` and `T-091`
   against `tests/Fixtures/google/`. `T-150` exists because two claims in
   `T-149` turned out to be documented but unobserved; find the others.
9. **Scope honesty.** Five vendors ship in beta (`D-018`) and storage is one
   of twelve streams. Say plainly whether that is deliverable, and if it is
   not, which single provider you would ship and what you would defer.

## Rules of engagement

- **Findings, not edits.** Change nothing. A `ready` spec is frozen by
  process, and a draft is not yours to rewrite either.
- **Everything in `decisions.md` is settled.** You may argue that one is
  wrong, but say it as a proposed decision record with its own reasoning, not
  as a correction.
- Do not review prose, the product vocabulary, or anything in the `design`
  stream. Do not propose a repository layer, a queue worker or an
  architecture change without tying it to a specific failure.
- Cite file and line for every finding.

## What to deliver

One document, in this directory, named as
`storage-2026-09-20-findings-<your name>.md`:

- Findings ordered by severity. Each says what breaks, the concrete scenario
  that breaks it, the file and line, and what you would do about it.
- Anything specified on documentation rather than an observed response,
  listed separately — each of those becomes a spike.
- The three things you would change first, and the three you would leave.
- What you could not evaluate, and what you would have needed.
