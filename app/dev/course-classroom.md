# Course classroom — the merged proposal and its research

Two independent proposals for a course taught live were written on 17 September 2026, one by the owner and one by Claude, neither having read the other. They were merged the same day into the decisions `D-024` to `D-031` and the [`classroom`](streams/classroom.md) stream, whose tasks are `T-122` to `T-145`. This file keeps what the decisions do not: the story the course tells, where Qori stands against it, what the two proposals agreed on and where they differed, the competitor patterns, the spike questions, the acceptance scenarios, the measures and the vendor facts. **The decisions and the task files are the authority wherever they differ from anything here**; this is the research that produced them, not a specification. The provisional task numbers both proposals used (0 to 12) are gone — every reference below is to a real task id.

## The story in Qori's nouns

Series, Episode, Peer, Group and the creator noun are configurable on an entitled plan (`App\Support\Terminology`, `useTerminology()`), so no screen may spell one out. "Session", "recording", "material", "homework" and "chat" are ordinary lang words and are not part of that vocabulary.

| The owner's course           | In Qori                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The course                   | One **Series**; each attendee holds an active **Access**                |
| Each weekly Zoom lesson      | One **live Episode**: a start, an end, a join link and its recordings   |
| Slides, PDFs, documents      | **Materials** on the Episode; course-wide items on the Series (`T-137`) |
| Homework                     | A material with role `homework`, a brief and a due date                 |
| The WhatsApp or WeChat group | A **chat invite** on the Series: a link or a QR image                   |
| An attendee anywhere         | A **Peer**                                                              |

A ten-week course is ten live Episodes, each with its own date and usually the same recurring meeting link. **Add the next session** (`T-134`) copies one to the same time seven days later. The product promise the owner wrote is the one to keep: **"One place to join class, catch up and find what you need."** Qori coordinates the creator's existing tools — the meeting keeps running on Zoom, the creator's files stay at their source, and Qori hosts the supported documents.

**One class, seen by a Peer fifteen hours behind the creator.** The creator is in Taipei and Lucía is in Los Angeles. Week 3 is Saturday 3 October 2026, 20:00–21:30 Taipei time, which is 12:00 UTC and 05:00–06:30 for Lucía.

### How Qori detects the end and the recording

**The end is arithmetic, not an observation.** `ends_at` is `starts_at` plus `length_minutes`, and `LiveSessionService::stateFor()` compares it with now (`D-026`). No vendor is asked whether the meeting finished, so a session that ran long, ran short or never happened still ends on the page when it was scheduled to. This is why the copy states the schedule and never says "live now" or "has ended".

**The recording is found by polling, never by webhook** (`D-027`). `qori:recordings:find` (`T-143`) walks the Episodes whose scheduled end has passed on a Zoom-connected Group, with backoff between checks, and **Check now** (`T-144`) runs the same search on demand. Webhooks were rejected because `recording.completed` reaching an unpublished user-managed app is unconfirmed (spike question 7) and a missed delivery would need the sweep anyway.

**The read is two steps, and both run every time** (`T-142`):

1. `GET /past_meetings/{meetingId}/instances` (MEDIUM) lists the ended instances, each with its own UUID and `start_time`. The brief read this only as a fallback; `T-142` reads it every time on purpose, because a meeting stopped and restarted is **two** instances and the shortcut alone can never return Part 1 (owner acceptance 8).
2. `GET /meetings/{uuid}/recordings` (LIGHT) reads each candidate instance's files.

**Double-encoding.** A meeting **id** in that path returns only the latest instance, so the UUID is what identifies one class. Every instance gets a new UUID, and a UUID beginning with `/` or containing `//` must be URL-encoded **twice** before it goes in the path — encoded once, Zoom routes it wrong.

**Only a completed video file is a recording.** A `recording_files[]` entry counts when its `status` is `completed` **and** it is the video file (`MP4`); `completed` is the only documented value (spike question 2 asks what an in-progress file reads as). A completed transcript, chat log or audio-only file is not something a Peer could open, so an instance holding only those is not a recording yet. **No completed video is the ordinary waiting case, not a failure** — it is what `waiting` means, and only the 48-hour `recording_wait_hours` boundary turns it into `overdue`.

**The link.** `share_url` where the payload carries it, otherwise `play_url` with the `recording_play_passcode` spliced in as `?pwd=`. `share_url` is documented in the `recording.completed` payload but **is not in the REST recordings schema** — whether REST returns it anyway is spike question 9, and until `T-122` answers it no task asserts either way.

**Matching, and what is held.** `D-027` matches on the instance start falling inside `[starts_at − 60 min, ends_at + 120 min]`; anything outside it, and any instance shorter than `min_recording_minutes`, is held for the creator rather than published — which is what stops a weekly class on one meeting id, or an early test recording, from silently attaching the wrong file.

**What the calls cost.** The recordings call is Zoom's LIGHT tier (30/s on Pro); past instances and user settings are MEDIUM (20/s on Pro). Limits are **per account and shared by every app installed on it**, so the backoff exists to be a good neighbour on a creator's account, not only to spare Qori.

### When there's no cloud recording

Cloud recording needs **Pro or above and a licensed host**. Local recording works on Basic, but only from the desktop app (Windows, macOS and Linux — not iOS, iPadOS or Android), and the file lands on the creator's own machine where Qori can never reach it.

**The Basic sentence, which every surface reduces to:** on Basic there is no cloud recording for Qori to find, so the creator records locally and pastes a link to wherever Peers can watch it. Nothing about this is a failure of Qori's, and no Peer-facing line names Zoom.

**The three Zoom fields** `T-141` reads from `GET /users/me/settings`, and stores under Qori's own names on the connection:

| Field                                         | What it settles                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `recording.cloud_recording`                   | Whether a recording can exist at all. `false` when the settings say so **or** when they could not be read and the account is not licensed; `null` only when neither is known |
| `recording.auto_recording`                    | Whether every meeting records without the host pressing anything — true when the value is `cloud`, a word resolved in the Zoom folder, never in `app/Support`                |
| `recording.authenticated_view_cloud_recoding` | Whether a Peer would be asked to sign in to watch. Zoom's own spelling, misspelling included, kept verbatim so one word means one fact from fixture to row to sweep          |

**The last two lines only render when `cloud_recording` is true.** Telling a Basic creator that automatic recording is off, when nothing could record anyway, is two facts where one is the point.

**At most three essential limits** sit above Connect for each of the five tiers (`ZoomBasic` through `ZoomBusinessPlus`). Basic's three are its lack of cloud recording, the meeting length that turns one class into two, and what Peers need to watch — a paid tier's are different, because a line shown on every tier has to be true on Basic too.

**What each side sees when the recording never arrives.** The Peer sees `waiting`, then `overdue` at 48 hours: "No recording has been added yet", with the honest resolution that an email follows if one is added. The creator is nudged at `creator_nudge_hours` (12 h where nothing was detected, 48 h where a connection is looking) and offered **Add the recording link** or **There's no recording for this session** — the second being a final state with no resolution, because it is true.

## What competitors do

**What Qori copies.**

- **One item from Join to Watch.** Teachable swaps "Join Meeting" for "Watch recording" in the same block. Maven's event moves from Join to a recording button. LearnWorlds shows that the session has finished until the replay arrives.
- **A recording matched to its session by time.** Disco reportedly takes the longest recording overlapping the event time, cloud recordings only (to confirm). A weekly class on one meeting id needs exactly this, which is why `D-027` matches on the instance start inside `[starts_at − 60 min, ends_at + 120 min]` and holds anything ambiguous.
- **Messages link to the platform, never the vendor.** Mighty Networks and Luma do this, so a replaced recording and the access check both survive.
- **The wait is stated honestly.** Maven says recordings can take a few hours and need Zoom Pro.
- **Everyone with access hears when the replay is ready**, not only those who attended. Maven emails Lightning Lesson recordings to signups; Livestorm sends a link to registrants who did not attend.
- **Both times are shown.** Luma shows the viewer's zone for online events and the event's own time with the local time in parentheses in lists. Maven sends calendar files. Qori leads with the Peer's zone and puts the Group's beside it.
- **Recipients are worked out at send time.** LearnWorlds' setup screen lists only current enrolments, but everyone enrolled when the reminder goes out receives it.
- **A chat invite behind access, with removal by hand.** MemberSpace (WhatsApp), Maven (an external community on course Home), Xiaoe-tech (a group QR on the payment-complete and post-purchase pages).
- **Several materials beside the session.** Teachable's lesson blocks; Circle's separate Files area with ordering and file metadata; Google Classroom attachments.
- **A creator recovery action and an availability-tied email.** Teachfloor's concluded lesson has a "Fetch recording from Zoom Cloud" action and students are emailed when the recording is available — which is `T-144`'s Check now and `T-128`'s notice.
- **Hold ambiguous or early test recordings.** Maven documents an early test recording replacing a future event's Join action. That is the concrete reason `D-027` holds a match outside the tolerance.

**What Qori avoids.**

- **Download and re-upload.** Skool records its own calls and the creator downloads within 14 days, then posts the file or re-uploads it; Podia replaces the Join button with a replay uploaded from a device or file library; Thinkific's documented path is a second Video Lesson or the Video Library (to confirm). It is a chore after every class, it puts video on Qori's storage against `project-plan.md` §8, and Thinkific's version needs a second lesson to hold the replay of the first.
- **Recordings unpublished by default in a separate list** (Zoom LTI Pro inside Canvas).
- **Replays gated on RSVP** (Circle; to confirm), which hides them from exactly the far-timezone Peers this stream exists for.
- **Hiding the timezone** (Thinkific reportedly does not show it to students; to confirm).
- **Copying vendor files in** (Teachable's uploader imports from Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive); `D-016` keeps content current at its source instead.
- **Grade books** (Google Classroom, Canvas), which are outside the north star.

**Zoom**

- **Webhooks:** a 3-second response; three retries (after 5, 20 and 60 minutes) on a 5xx, a timeout or a connection failure, never on a 3xx or 4xx; `endpoint.url_validation`; `x-zm-signature`.     - documented fields include `recording_play_passcode` (Zoom documents splicing it into `play_url` or `share_url` with `?pwd=`), `auto_delete_date`, and per file `play_url` and `status`, whose only documented value is `completed`;
